Category 07 — Media Centers

Dead space, turned into a wall you actually want to look at.

An empty alcove or an awkward wall is the most common starting point for a media center — PS Woodworking builds furniture-grade, wall-to-wall cabinetry that hides the cords and components and leaves the columns and detail work to do the talking.

Discuss Your Media Center View the Gallery
01 / Detail Work

Round column capitals with an arched surround

Turned columns and a coped arch frame the television opening — the kind of detail that reads as architecture, not electronics furniture.

02 / Component Access

Flip-down drawer fronts for easy component access

Hardware built into the drawer fronts themselves means remotes and receivers stay reachable without ever being visible.

03 / French Country

A built-in with a distressed, furniture-grade finish

Distressed French Country styling applied to a fully built-in unit — proof that "built-in" doesn't have to mean modern or minimal.

How It Works

From idea to install.

Media centers have to plan around real electronics — ventilation, cable routing, and clearances are worked out before the design is finalized.

01

Consultation & Drawings

Measure the wall or alcove, discuss component sizes and access needs — leave with a real drawing.

02

Layout & Ventilation Planning

Cable routing, ventilation, and door mechanisms (retractable, flip-down, bi-fold) planned before construction begins.

03

Shop-Built, Hand-Finished

Built and finished in the shop, with column and arch detail work fit and test-assembled before delivery.

04

Installation

Set into the alcove or wall, scribed for a seamless fit, and finished on-site.
